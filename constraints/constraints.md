## [Flood] Freeboard (height above BFE)
**Status:** Verified

**Rule:** Florida Building Code requires 1 ft above BFE. Longboat Key requires 3 ft above BFE in V-zones and seaward of the CCCL, or FDEP's elevation if that is higher. Ordinance 2024-03 lets owners add 2 ft more anywhere in town, and Ordinance 2024 (June) allows up to 5 ft total on low-lying lots (at or below 3.5 ft NAVD88).

**For a hardened compound:** On a Gulf-front V-zone lot, plan for the lowest structural member at BFE + 3 ft at least, or FDEP's number. Optional extra freeboard can lower insurance costs, but it counts against the height limit.

**Notes:** The V-zone 3 ft rule is from the Town's own FAQ. Confirm the exact current wording in LBK Code Chapter 154.

**Sources:** [LBK FAQ: Freeboard Rule](https://www.longboatkey.org/m/faq); [LBK Ordinance 2024-03 (Municode)](https://library.municode.com/fl/longboat_key/ordinances/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=2024); [Your Observer: optional 5 ft freeboard](https://www.yourobserver.com/news/2024/jun/12/longboat-ordinance-resilient-construction/)

## [Flood] 50% substantial improvement / substantial damage rule
**Status:** NEEDS VERIFICATION

**Rule:** If repairs or improvements cost 50% or more of the building's market value (structure only, not land), the whole building must be brought up to current flood rules, including elevation.

**For a hardened compound:** Renovating an older, low house is limited by the 50% cap. A full rebuild on piles is often the realistic route to a hardened compound. Some towns add up the cost of work across several years, so check whether LBK does.

**Notes:** The 50% threshold is the federal NFIP rule and is verified. Did not confirm whether LBK Chapter 154 counts costs cumulatively (a lookback period).

**Sources:** [FEMA Substantial Improvement/Substantial Damage Desk Reference (P-758)](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_p-758-substantial-improvement-substantial-damage-desk-reference.pdf); [LBK Code Ch. 154 Flood Control (Municode)](https://library.municode.com/fl/longboat_key/codes/code_of_ordinances)

## [Flood] V-zone foundations: open piles/columns and breakaway walls
**Status:** Verified

**Rule:** In V zones, buildings must sit on piles or columns with the lowest horizontal structural member at or above the design flood elevation. Fill can't be used for structural support. Any enclosed space below must use breakaway walls, and it is limited to parking, building access, and storage.

**For a hardened compound:** The ground floor can't be a fortified concrete level. Hardening goes into the elevated structure, and the area under the house stays open or breakaway.

**Notes:** Standard NFIP/FBC R322.3 requirement. Coastal A zones may have similar local rules.

**Sources:** [FEMA Coastal Construction Manual P-55](https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/risk-management/building-science/coastal-construction-manual); [FBC Residential 2023, R322.3 Coastal high-hazard areas](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/FLRC2023P1/chapter-3-building-planning#FLRC2023P1_Pt03_Ch03_SecR322.3)

## [Coastal] CCCL permit from FDEP (seaward of the Coastal Construction Control Line)
**Status:** Verified

**Rule:** Construction or excavation seaward of the CCCL needs an FDEP permit under s.161.053, F.S., and Chapter 62B-33, F.A.C. Under s.161.053, most new major habitable structures also can't go seaward of the 30-year erosion projection.

**For a hardened compound:** A Gulf-front lot almost certainly needs a CCCL permit. FDEP sets its own structural, elevation, and siting standards and can limit how far seaward you build.

**Notes:** Check where the CCCL runs on this parcel using the FDEP CCCL GIS viewer.

**Sources:** [FDEP Coastal Construction Control Line Program](https://floridadep.gov/rcp/coastal-construction-control-line); [Florida Statutes s.161.053](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0161/Sections/0161.053.html); [Rule 62B-33 F.A.C.](https://www.flrules.org/gateway/ChapterHome.asp?Chapter=62B-33)

## [Coastal] Gulf waterfront yard: 150 ft from mean high water / ECL
**Status:** Verified

**Rule:** Under LBK Code 158.094, lots that touch the Gulf must keep a 150-ft Gulf waterfront yard. It is measured from the mean high-water line, or from the Erosion Control Line where one has been set. Structures generally can't be built in it.

**For a hardened compound:** The buildable area of the 0.71-acre lot can be much smaller than it looks. Map the MHWL/ECL and the 150-ft line before you assume a layout.

**Notes:** Text read from a mirror of the code. Confirm on Municode and check for any listed exceptions (e.g., dune walkovers).

**Sources:** [LBK Code 158.094 Yard regulations (Municode)](https://library.municode.com/fl/longboat_key/codes/code_of_ordinances); [Zoneomics copy of 158.094](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/longboat-key-FL/chapter_5)

## [Coastal] Seawall / coastal armoring permitting and height
**Status:** Verified

**Rule:** LBK Ordinance 2024-04 (April 1, 2024) raised the maximum seawall height from 4.5 ft to 6 ft NAVD88, including the cap (Code 151.03). Raising a wall 6 inches or more needs a drainage assessment. On the Gulf, state (FDEP) permits come first, and armoring is generally allowed only when an inhabited structure is threatened.

**For a hardened compound:** A 6-ft NAVD88 max is far below storm surge, so a seawall can't serve as flood defense. On the Gulf beach, new armoring is hard to get approved.

**Notes:** The cited 6 ft NAVD88 max matches the 2024 ordinance (it replaced 4.5 ft). Read 151.03 directly to confirm the details for Gulf-front walls vs. canal walls.

**Sources:** [LBK Code Ch. 151 (Municode)](https://library.municode.com/fl/longboat_key/codes/code_of_ordinances); [Your Observer: seawalls up to 6 ft](https://www.yourobserver.com/news/2024/apr/10/seawall-height-longboat-key/); [FDEP Coastal armoring (CCCL)](https://floridadep.gov/rcp/coastal-construction-control-line)

## [Wind] HVHZ applicability
**Status:** Verified

**Rule:** The High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) provisions of the FBC apply only in Miami-Dade and Broward counties.

**For a hardened compound:** HVHZ is not required in Manatee. You can choose to specify HVHZ/Miami-Dade NOA-approved products as a hardening upgrade.

**Notes:** The HVHZ definition (Miami-Dade and Broward only) is long-standing FBC text.

**Sources:** [FBC 2023 Building, Section 1620 HVHZ (ICC)](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/FLBC2023P1)

## [Wind] Wind-borne debris region and impact-rated openings
**Status:** Verified

**Rule:** FBC defines the wind-borne debris region as areas within 1 mile of the coast where Vult is 130 mph or more, or anywhere Vult is 140 mph or more. In that region, glazed openings need impact-rated products or approved shutters (tested to ASTM E1996/E1886 or similar).

**For a hardened compound:** All windows and doors should be impact-rated, with Florida Product Approval. Larger missile levels (e.g., Level D/E) are a sensible upgrade.

**Notes:** A Gulf-front site is within 1 mile of the mean high water line, so it falls in the WBDR whatever the exact Vult.

**Sources:** [FBC 2023 Section 1609 / R301.2.1.2 (ICC)](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/FLBC2023P1); [Florida Product Approval](https://www.floridabuilding.org/pr/pr_app_srch.aspx); [FGDL wind-speed data history (WBDR definition)](https://fgdl.org/content/pdfs/Florida_Wind_Speed_FGDL_GIS_Data_History_20240722.pdf)

## [Structure] ICF / reinforced CMU walls
**Status:** NEEDS VERIFICATION

**Rule:** Concrete walls must meet FBC Chapters 19 and 21 (ACI 318 and TMS 402/602). Residential ICF can use FBC-R R608 or an engineered design. In V zones, walls below the DFE must be breakaway, not structural.

**For a hardened compound:** ICF or grouted, reinforced CMU is a solid choice for elevated living floors. It can't be used as a solid ground-floor flood wall in a V zone.

**Notes:** Section references are from FBC structure. Specific rebar or thickness numbers were not pulled and need an engineer.

## [Environment] Sea turtle-safe lighting
**Status:** NEEDS VERIFICATION

**Rule:** LBK Code Title 9, Chapter 100 (Marine Turtle Protection, amended by Ord. 2021-01) requires shielded, downward, long-wavelength 'turtle-friendly' lighting not visible from the beach, and tinted glass on new beachfront development. FWC and FDEP set the statewide model rules, and CCCL permits impose lighting conditions. Nesting season is May 1 to Oct 31.

**For a hardened compound:** Exterior and security lighting for the compound must be FWC-certified, low, and shielded. Window tint (45% transmittance or less is typical) may be required. Floodlit perimeter security on the beach side isn't allowed.

**Notes:** Chapter and ordinance confirmed. The window transmittance number and season dates come from the typical FWC model ordinance and should be confirmed in LBK 100.03.

**Sources:** [LBK Code Ch.100 Marine Turtle Protection](https://library.municode.com/fl/longboat_key/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT9GERE_CH100MATUPR); [LBK Marine Turtle Protection page](https://www.longboatkey.org/214/Marine-Turtle-Protection); [FWC Sea Turtle Lighting Ordinances](https://myfwc.com/conservation/you-conserve/lighting/ordinances/)

## [Zoning] 'Structure/building' definition and the 6-inch rule
**Status:** Verified

**Rule:** LBK 158.144: a 'Building' is any roofed structure, including a screened pool cage. 'Any other structural appendage to a building, including a deck which protrudes more than six inches above finished grade, shall be considered a building, except that permeable planters shall not be considered a building.' The Town's coverage worksheet counts anything more than 6 inches above finished or adjacent grade.

**For a hardened compound:** Raised terraces, walls, stairs, and equipment pads more than 6 in. above grade count toward coverage and yard limits. Keep site hardscape at grade where you can. The definition was amended in 2024 and 2025 (Ord. 2024-07, 2025-03), so recheck it.

**Notes:** The quoted wording is from a mirror of 158.144. Recent amendments may have changed it, so check the current Municode text.

**Sources:** [LBK Code 158.144 Definitions (Municode)](https://library.municode.com/fl/longboat_key/codes/code_of_ordinances); [LBK Single-Family Lot Coverage Worksheet](https://www.longboatkey.org/DocumentCenter/View/161/Single-Family-Lot-Coverage-Calculation-Worksheet-PDF)

## [Insurance] NFIP, private flood, Citizens, wind, elevation certificate
**Status:** NEEDS VERIFICATION

**Rule:** NFIP caps residential building coverage at $250k, so higher-value homes need private excess flood. Under Florida law (s.627.351(6), F.S.), Citizens policies need flood coverage. Wind is a separate peril in Florida. An Elevation Certificate is required for permits and is key to pricing. LBK is in the NFIP Community Rating System.

**For a hardened compound:** Build high (freeboard) and hardened (FORTIFIED, wind mitigation credits) to control premiums. Get quotes before closing.

**Notes:** The NFIP $250k cap is verified federal policy. Citizens eligibility and the timing of its flood mandate, plus LBK's CRS class, were not confirmed this session.

**Sources:** [FEMA NFIP](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance); [Citizens Property Insurance flood requirement](https://www.citizensfla.com/flood-insurance-requirement); [FEMA Elevation Certificate](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance/find-form/underwriting/elevation-certificates); [LBK elevation certificate search](https://www.longboatkey.org/224/Flood-Risk-Elevation-Certificate-Search)
